# GPTBot Market — positioning, marketing and growth audit

**Date:** 2026-08-01
**Mode:** strategy and audit only; no campaign, outreach or tracking mutation performed

## Commercial verdict

Marketing should not try to create demand for a “marketplace” yet. There is no real supply, transaction history, category proof or repeatable service operation. The immediate marketing job is narrower: recruit one credible seller, seed a small set of real buyer tasks, document the truth, and learn whether a grounded Telegram shortlist produces qualified demand.

Current marketing maturity is **2.5/10 (`черновик`)**. The website exists and SEO foundations are real, but positioning, naming, proof, creative, attribution, retention and monetization validation are not yet a system.

## Positioning

### Category

**A Telegram product-finding assistant for connected local store catalogs.**

Avoid presenting GPTBot Market as:

- a public marketplace;
- a payment/escrow platform;
- an all-knowing AI shopping agent;
- a self-service store builder;
- a replacement for the seller;
- a national Uzum/OLX competitor.

### Canonical buyer promise

> **«Напишите, что Вам нужно, — GPTBot найдёт подходящие товары в каталогах подключённых магазинов.»**

Supporting proof:

- price and availability come from the connected catalog;
- the shortlist is intentionally small;
- if facts are missing, GPTBot says so or asks the seller;
- the order step creates a request and does not take payment.

### Canonical seller promise

> **“Bring a verified catalog; GPTBot helps buyers find the right items and sends you the requests or questions that need a person.”**

Do not promise “more sales” until a pilot measures incremental qualified demand.

### Naming architecture

| Layer | Name | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Company/master brand | GPTBot | trust, team, product family |
| Buyer product | GPTBot Market | bot, buyer landing, social, referrals |
| Seller program | Sotuvchi by GPTBot | seller acquisition, onboarding, education |
| Domain | GPTBot.uz | company, legal, support and trust center |

Owner and legal/domain review are required before rollout. Do not rename production in this audit phase.

## Audience hierarchy

### Supply first

1. **Pilot seller:** verified owner-operated shop; 10–30 visually presentable, frequently asked-about items; price/stock can be maintained; responds in Telegram within an agreed SLA.
2. **Pilot staff/operator:** person who updates catalog and answers requests daily, even if not the owner.
3. **Expansion seller:** similar category and fulfillment model after the first case proves usefulness.

### Demand second

1. **High-intent buyer:** has a need, budget or constraints but not an exact item.
2. **Comparison buyer:** deciding among 2–3 known options.
3. **Repeat buyer:** knows the store/category and wants faster reordering or updated availability.

### Not a target now

- broad bargain browsing with no intent;
- nationwide multi-category discovery;
- regulated/high-risk goods;
- sellers with stale stock, no responsible operator or no consent;
- businesses demanding payments, escrow, delivery fleet or CRM integration as Pilot #1 prerequisites.

## Pilot offers

### Seller Pilot #1 offer

**Offer:** an assisted, verified pilot for one store and one constrained catalog. GPTBot team helps validate/import the catalog, configures the Telegram storefront path, observes buyer tasks and reviews results with the seller.

**Seller supplies:** consent, verified owner/operator, 10–30 approved products, integer UZS prices, opening stock baseline, delivery/fulfillment terms, product photos, response SLA and named contacts outside Git.

**GPTBot supplies:** safe catalog import/QA, grounded buyer flow, order-request and human-question routing, privacy-safe measurement, daily review and pause/incident process.

**Pilot does not include:** payment acceptance, escrow, Click/Payme, logistics, public marketplace listing, guaranteed leads/sales, automated publication or seller self-service.

**Success review:** useful-result rate, zero-result causes, request completion, seller response time, stock correction count, qualified request quality, seller workload and willingness to continue/pay.

### Buyer seed offer

“Try one real shopping need in Telegram. GPTBot will return a small selection from the connected pilot catalog or honestly say it cannot.”

No discount is necessary until value is observed. Incentives can contaminate intent and seller-value measurement.

## Seven cold-start questions

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| 1. Why will the first seller join without buyers? | Assisted setup, no public-scale commitment, structured feedback and a chance to turn existing Telegram inquiries into measurable catalog requests. Recruitment must come through trusted founder/partner outreach, not ads. |
| 2. Why will the first buyer use it with one store? | Only if the category/store already has demand and the assistant reduces decision effort. Frame it as that store’s smarter catalog, not a broad market. |
| 3. Which side comes first? | Supply quality first, then a deliberately seeded buyer cohort. Empty discovery is fatal. |
| 4. How do we avoid fake liquidity? | Label the closed pilot, connected stores and synthetic/demo content. Never mix synthetic products into real buyer acquisition. |
| 5. What is the smallest network effect? | One category with multiple trusted catalogs where a single need can yield a better shortlist than one store alone. Do not claim this before Store #2+. |
| 6. How do we reach buyers cheaply? | Seller-owned Telegram/Instagram audiences, QR/link at the store, partner communities and high-intent SEO examples; all tagged by source. |
| 7. What makes them return? | Fresh stock, faster decision, reliable seller response and useful saved context—not AI novelty. Retention features come after these basics hold. |

## Content pillars

1. **Show the task:** real need → constraints → grounded shortlist.
2. **Catalog truth:** where price/stock come from, what “last verified” means, how correction works.
3. **Human when needed:** a seller answers the complex question; show response expectation.
4. **Seller operating value:** fewer repetitive questions, clearer request, visible daily exceptions.
5. **Pilot transparency:** what exists, what does not, what was learned and changed.
6. **Local buying literacy:** how to compare specs, delivery and merchant responsibility without pretending GPTBot guarantees the sale.

Every content item must have a functional CTA and a source-of-truth owner. The production list is in `GPTBOT_MARKET_CREATIVE_MATRIX.md`.

## Telegram roadmap

### Before Pilot #1

- Configure approved public name, avatar, description, commands and RU/UZ media preview set through an authorized owner action.
- Pin a 20–30 second truthful demo and one example query.
- Deep links must distinguish buyer start, seller pilot information and specific consented storefront; no link may grant authority.
- Create a support/pilot contact expectation without exposing private operator identity unnecessarily.

### During pilot

- Seller posts one launch explanation and three task examples.
- GPTBot publishes a short pilot diary: useful queries, honest zero results, catalog corrections and response behavior—aggregated, consented and PII-free.
- Weekly seller education: stock freshness, photo quality, response SLA and what creates useful matches.

### After proof

- Referral/share flow for a result or storefront, with source attribution and abuse limits.
- Category digest only with opt-in and fresh catalog facts.
- Lifecycle prompts based on explicit user action, never raw private-text profiling.

## Instagram roadmap

- Reels: “one need, three constraints, two products” screen demo.
- Carousel: “what GPTBot knows / does not know”.
- Stories: question sticker feeding anonymized example searches; never publish raw user text.
- Seller proof: operator explains catalog update and response workflow.
- Pilot outcome card: denominator, period, store/category and limitations visible.
- RU and UZ are separately authored; do not put both languages into cramped frames by default.

Success is not views. Measure qualified bot starts, useful results, product views and order requests by tagged creative.

## SEO roadmap

The current `/ru/sotuvchi/` and `/uz/sotuvchi/` pages have a good technical shell but material truth gaps. First correct claims and consolidate intent. Then build only evidence-supported pages:

- “поиск товаров в Telegram” / UZ equivalent;
- category pilot pages once a real category exists;
- “как работает заказ-заявка”;
- “как GPTBot проверяет цену и наличие”;
- seller pilot qualification and catalog preparation;
- real case study after consent and measured outcome.

Use schema/FAQ only for visible, truthful content. Do not generate city/category pages before supply exists. Root GPTBot.uz lead-bot pages and GPTBot Market shopping intent need deliberate internal-link and message separation.

## Demo, case study and pilot story

### Demo script

1. Buyer writes a real constraint-led need.
2. GPTBot reflects key constraints.
3. Two grounded products appear with photos, price, availability, store and reason-to-fit.
4. Buyer compares one important trade-off.
5. Buyer submits an order request; “not payment” is visible.
6. Seller receives and responds within the agreed SLA.
7. End card states limits and connected catalog source.

### Case study template

- Seller/category/context and consent.
- Baseline inquiry process and response time.
- Catalog size, freshness rule and pilot cohort.
- Funnel with denominators: starts → searches → useful results → product views → requests → seller replies.
- Zero-result and stock-correction lessons.
- Seller workload and buyer qualitative feedback.
- What GPTBot did not handle.
- Decision: stop, iterate or expand.

Avoid vanity metrics, percentage lifts on tiny denominators and “AI increased sales” without causal evidence.

## Acquisition and retention model

### Acquisition funnel

**Seller:** referred lead → qualified → consented → verified → catalog accepted → activated → responsive → value review → continue/pay.

**Buyer:** tagged exposure → bot start → need expressed → useful result → product view/compare → request/handoff → seller response → outcome → qualified return.

Required breakdowns: locale, category, store, source and new/returning status. Never put raw query, phone or conversation content into analytics.

### Retention

Prioritize operational retention before notification features:

- seller keeps stock/photo/catalog quality current;
- seller responds within SLA;
- buyer receives honest status and outcome;
- zero-result reasons feed catalog improvement;
- saved context or reorder is added only after consent and observed repeat need.

Core early retention measures:

- seller active days with an operational task completed;
- percentage of catalog kept within freshness policy;
- buyer 7/30-day qualified return, not any `/start`;
- repeat useful-result and repeat request rate;
- seller continuation and willingness to pay.

## Monetization hypotheses and validation

### What not to monetize now

- no marketplace take rate without payment/attribution/merchant framework;
- no buyer fee before repeat utility;
- no pay-to-rank or sponsored result that compromises relevance;
- no “AI token” pricing language;
- no Click/Payme integration solely to unlock a fee.

### Hypotheses to test

| Hypothesis | Payer | Value metric | Test | Evidence required before price |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Assisted setup fee | seller | accepted catalog + activated storefront | quote after discovery to 5–8 qualified sellers | setup effort/COGS and willingness to pay |
| Monthly pilot subscription | seller | active store with catalog, routing and basic report | price-sensitivity interview + renewal decision after pilot | qualified requests, saved workload, reliability |
| Usage tier later | seller | qualified requests or active catalog size | shadow billing only, no charge | metric cannot be gamed and correlates with seller value |
| Premium operations later | seller | faster support, multi-operator, advanced analytics | concept test with proven active sellers | operational cost and demand |
| Transaction fee | seller | completed paid order | defer | payments, merchant-of-record, returns/disputes, attribution and legal approval |

### Validation sequence

1. Interview 8–12 qualified sellers about current inquiry volume, lost demand, catalog upkeep, response workload and existing spend.
2. Present the assisted pilot with a realistic non-zero price anchor; record reasons, not just yes/no.
3. Measure GPTBot service COGS: onboarding/import/QA/support/incident and runtime cost.
4. At pilot review, ask for a continuation choice at two concrete packages.
5. Only after 3+ comparable stores, define a standard offer and acquisition economics.

## Growth gates

| Gate | Minimum proof |
|---|---|
| Recruit Pilot #1 | verified consented seller, responsible operator, accepted catalog package, support/SLA owners |
| Seed buyers | no synthetic listings in real cohort; tagged seller-owned distribution; truthful landing |
| Expand to Store #2 | Pilot #1 useful-result and response loop works; no unresolved P0 incident |
| Start paid acquisition | repeatable conversion baseline, approved creative, attribution and known seller capacity |
| Launch referral | real repeat value, consent, fraud/abuse handling and source tracking |
| Public launch | category liquidity, retention, operations, trust center and commercial terms pass roadmap Stage 10 |
